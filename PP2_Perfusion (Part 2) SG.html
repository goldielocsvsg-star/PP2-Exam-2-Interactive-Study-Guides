🫀 Normal Cardiovascular Physiology

Cardiac Output — The Foundational Formula

CO = HR × SV
Cardiac Output = Heart Rate × Stroke Volume  |  Normal: 4–8 L/min

Every intervention in cardiac care either affects HR or SV (or both). Understanding which drugs alter which component is the foundation of cardiac pharmacology.

The Five Determinants of Cardiac Output

DeterminantDefinitionIf ↑If ↓
PreloadVentricular stretch before contraction; end-diastolic volume (EDV)↑SV via Starling's Law — to a point↓SV → ↓CO → inadequate perfusion
AfterloadResistance the ventricle overcomes to eject blood (SVR)↓SV — harder to pump against resistance↑SV — less work required to eject
ContractilityIntrinsic myocardial force independent of preload (inotropy)↑SV regardless of fill volume↓SV → HF, cardiogenic shock
Heart RateBeats per minute (normal 60–100 bpm)↑CO initially; beyond ~150 bpm diastolic fill time too short↓CO → fatigue, hypoperfusion
Ejection Fraction (EF)% of end-diastolic volume ejected per beatNormal ≥55% = adequate pump function<40% = systolic heart failure; <55% = reduced function

Starling's Law — The Critical Concept

The greater the myocardial stretch (↑ preload), the stronger the subsequent contraction — up to an optimal point. Beyond the optimal stretch, the overstretched muscle generates less force.

⚠
Clinical application: IV fluids ↑ preload → ↑ CO in hypovolemia. In heart failure, the ventricle is already overstretched — adding fluids worsens failure. This is why we give diuretics (↓ preload) in HF, not fluids.

Neurohormonal Regulation

Sympathetic (Adrenergic)

  • Releases epinephrine + norepinephrine
  • ↑ HR, ↑ contractility, ↑ vasoconstriction → ↑ BP and CO
  • Beta-blockers antagonize this system

Parasympathetic (Vagal)

  • Acetylcholine → ↓ HR, vasodilation
  • Dominant at rest; protects against excessive tachycardia

RAAS (Long-term BP Control)

  • Angiotensin II → vasoconstriction → ↑ BP
  • Aldosterone → Na/H₂O retention → ↑ preload + ↑ BP
  • ADH → ↑ water reabsorption → ↑ blood volume
  • ACE inhibitors and ARBs block this system

Blood Pressure Formula

BP = CO × SVR
↗ High-Yield Takeaways — Physiology
  • CO = HR × SV. Memorize this. Every cardiac drug affects one or both components.
  • Preload = filling (stretch). Afterload = resistance. Reduce both in HF to reduce myocardial workload.
  • EF ≥55% = normal. EF <40% = systolic heart failure. EF <55% = borderline/reduced.
  • Starling's Law: more stretch = stronger contraction, until the muscle is overstretched — then force drops.
  • RAAS = long-term BP. Sympathetic = acute BP. Both are targets for cardiac pharmacology.

↗ High-Yield Takeaways — PVD
  • 6 Ps present → limb-threatening emergency → heparin + surgical intervention STAT. Report all 6 Ps immediately.
  • PAD ulcers: toes, well-defined borders. Venous ulcers: inner ankle, irregular borders. Know this cold.
  • PAD = cold, pulseless, pale. Venous = warm, reddish-brown staining, edema.
  • Dependent rubor (red when hanging, pale when elevated) = severe PAD sign.
  • Cilostazol: no grapefruit, no smoking. Absolute CI in heart failure (Black Box).
  • Intermittent claudication: pain with walking, relieved by rest. Rest pain = critical ischemia = emergency.

Risk Factors for CAD / ACS

Modifiable

  • Dyslipidemia (↑LDL, ↓HDL)
  • Hypertension
  • Cigarette smoking
  • Diabetes mellitus / insulin resistance
  • Obesity / metabolic syndrome
  • Sedentary lifestyle
  • Atherogenic diet (high salt, saturated fat, trans-fat)

Non-Modifiable

  • Advanced age
  • Male sex or women post-menopause
  • Family history of premature CAD

Target Lipid Values

  • Total cholesterol: <200 mg/dL
  • LDL: <130 mg/dL (post-MI: <70)
  • HDL: men >45; women >55 mg/dL
  • Triglycerides: men 40–160 / women 35–135

Cardiogenic Shock — Report Immediately

Occurs when >40% of the left ventricle is necrotic → pump failure → end-organ hypoperfusion. Carries high mortality if unrecognized.

Sign / SymptomClinical Threshold
HypotensionSBP <90 mmHg OR >30 mmHg below patient's baseline
TachycardiaHR >100 bpm (compensatory response)
Urine output<0.5–1 mL/kg/hr (renal hypoperfusion)
Skin perfusionCold, clammy; poor peripheral pulses
NeurologicAgitation, confusion, restlessness (cerebral hypoperfusion)
RespiratoryTachypnea; pulmonary congestion (↑ LVEDP backing up)
ChestContinuing chest discomfort despite treatment

ACS Priority Problems — In Clinical Order

1
Acute Pain — O₂ supply/demand imbalance
→ Oxygen if SpO₂ <90%, NTG SL, morphine if unresponsive to NTG, aspirin
2
↓ Myocardial Tissue Perfusion — arterial flow interrupted
→ PCI or tPA; heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel (dual antiplatelet)
3
Potential Dysrhythmias — ischemia + ventricular irritability
→ Continuous cardiac monitoring, correct electrolytes, antidysrhythmics as ordered
4
Potential Heart Failure — left ventricular dysfunction
→ Beta-blockers within 24 hrs, ACEIs within 24 hrs, diuretics if fluid overloaded

↗ High-Yield Takeaways — ACS / MI
  • Pathophysiology sequence: plaque rupture → platelet aggregation → clot → ischemia → necrosis.
  • STEMI = 100% occlusion + ST elevation. NSTEMI = partial occlusion + ST depression/T-wave changes.
  • tPA (alteplase) is ONLY for STEMI. Giving it to an NSTEMI patient = contraindicated medication error.
  • Time targets are survival targets: PCI ≤90 min, tPA ≤30 min. These are not flexible guidelines.
  • Cardiogenic shock: SBP <90, cold clammy, UO <0.5 mL/kg/hr, confusion. REPORT IMMEDIATELY.
  • LVEDP↑ = ventricle can't empty → blood backs up into pulmonary circulation → dyspnea/crackles.

Nitroglycerin SL Protocol — At Home and In Hospital

At Home Protocol

Angina begins → 1 SL NTG (0.4 mg)
wait 5 min
Pain persists → 2nd SL NTG
wait 5 min
Pain persists → 3rd SL NTG
wait 5 min
Still no relief → CALL 911. Do NOT drive yourself.

In-Hospital Protocol

  • NTG 0.4 mg SL q5 min × 3 doses
  • Assess for IV NTG if discomfort, HTN, or pulmonary congestion persist
  • IV NTG: use glass bottle + manufacturer tubing

Hold NTG If:

  • SBP <90 mmHg
  • HR <50 bpm or >100 bpm
  • Sildenafil (Viagra), tadalafil, or vardenafil within 24 hours

Storage

  • Original dark glass container; room temperature
  • Check expiration dates; replace regularly
  • Remove transdermal patches at bedtime (prevents tolerance)

Non-Pharmacologic Angina Management

Avoid Triggers

  • Heavy meals (↑ cardiac demand post-prandially)
  • Smoking (nicotine causes vasoconstriction)
  • Extreme temperature changes
  • Unaccustomed or strenuous exercise
  • Emotional stress and anger

Promote Cardiovascular Health

  • Heart-healthy diet (low salt, low saturated fat)
  • Regular moderate exercise (as tolerated)
  • Adequate sleep and stress management
  • Strict medication adherence
  • Cardiac rehabilitation program
↗ High-Yield Takeaways — Angina
  • Stable angina: exertion-induced, relieved by rest/NTG. Unstable: rest pain = ACS = ED immediately.
  • All three antianginal classes reduce cardiac workload — through different but complementary mechanisms.
  • NTG + sildenafil (Viagra/Cialis/Levitra) = absolute contraindication. Always ask before administering NTG.
  • At home: NTG ×1 → 5 min wait → ×2 → 5 min → ×3 → 5 min → call 911. Do not take 3 and then call.
  • Atypical symptoms (jaw, shoulder, indigestion) are the presentation in women, elderly, and diabetics. Do not miss them.
  • Do NOT combine verapamil or diltiazem with beta-blockers — compounding bradycardia and AV block risk.

STEMI — At a Glance

  • 100% coronary occlusion
  • ST elevation ≥2 contiguous leads
  • Transmural necrosis (full thickness)
  • Troponin elevated early
  • PCI ≤90 min (door-to-balloon)
  • tPA ≤30 min if PCI unavailable
  • ~30% of all MIs

NSTEMI — At a Glance

  • Partial coronary occlusion
  • ST depression / T-wave inversion
  • Subendocardial ischemia
  • Troponin rises 3–12 hrs later
  • Medical management first; PCI risk-stratified
  • tPA NEVER indicated
  • ~70% of all MIs

Chest Pain — Clinical Decision Algorithm

1. Assess: VS, SpO₂, pain (OPQRST), diaphoresis, radiation. Obtain 12-lead ECG STAT.
2. Notify provider. Activate STEMI protocol if ST elevation present in ≥2 contiguous leads.
3. Establish IV access. Prepare: Morphine · Oxygen · Nitroglycerin · Aspirin (MONA)
4. Aspirin 162–325 mg CHEWED immediately (unless contraindicated)
5. O₂ via NC if SpO₂ <90%. NTG SL if BP allows (SBP >90, no sildenafil). Morphine if pain persists after NTG.
6. STEMI confirmed → PCI within 90 min. If PCI unavailable in 90 min → alteplase within 30 min.
7. Continue: Heparin · Clopidogrel · Beta-blocker (within 24 hrs) · ACEI (within 24 hrs) · Statin
8. Monitor: Continuous cardiac rhythm · BP · HR · UO · Neurologic status · Troponin trends · aPTT

  • Know your baselines. Know your hold thresholds. Know your antidotes.
  • Time is muscle — every minute of coronary occlusion is irreversible myocardium.
