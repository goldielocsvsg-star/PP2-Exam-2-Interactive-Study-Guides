QUESTION 01 Multiple Choice
During the coagulation cascade, the intrinsic and extrinsic pathways converge at a common point. Which factor marks this convergence?
AFactor VII
BFactor X
CThrombin (Factor IIa)
DFibrinogen
📋 Rationale
B is correct. The extrinsic (tissue factor) and intrinsic (contact activation) pathways converge at Factor X, forming the common pathway. The formula 3 + 7 = 10 helps recall that factors III and VII of the extrinsic pathway lead to Factor X. Factor VII is specific to the extrinsic pathway only. Thrombin (IIa) and fibrinogen are downstream products of the common pathway, not the convergence point itself.
QUESTION 02 Multiple Choice
A client is prescribed heparin for DVT treatment. Which laboratory value does the nurse monitor to evaluate therapeutic effectiveness?
AINR 2.0–3.0
BPlatelet count > 150,000/mm³
CaPTT 60–80 seconds (1.5–2× normal)
DFactor Xa level within 0.5–1.0 IU/mL
📋 Rationale
C is correct. Heparin therapy is monitored with the aPTT. The therapeutic range is 60–80 seconds, which represents 1.5–2× the normal value of ~40 seconds. aPTT is monitored every 4–6 hours during therapy. INR monitors warfarin, not heparin. Factor Xa levels monitor LMWH (enoxaparin), not unfractionated heparin. Platelet count is monitored for HIT but does not indicate anticoagulant effectiveness.
QUESTION 03 Priority
Clinical Scenario

AApply ice pack to the ecchymotic site
BReduce the heparin infusion rate by half
CDiscontinue the heparin infusion and notify the provider immediately
DAdminister acetaminophen for the headache and recheck aPTT in 4 hours
📋 Rationale
C is correct. An aPTT of 180 seconds is supratherapeutic (therapeutic is 60–80 sec). Combined with a new headache and ecchymosis, this picture suggests active bleeding, potentially intracranial. Simply reducing the rate (B) is too slow a response for a supratherapeutic level with bleeding signs. Ice pack (A) and acetaminophen (D) do not address the root emergency of heparin toxicity.

QUESTION 05 Pharmacology
A client's warfarin dose was increased two weeks ago. Today the INR is 4.8. Which medication should the nurse anticipate administering?
AProtamine sulfate IV
BVitamin K
CIdarucizumab (Praxbind)
DAminocaproic acid
📋 Rationale
B is correct. Vitamin K is the antidote for warfarin overdose. It replaces the vitamin K that warfarin has suppressed, restoring production of clotting factors VII, IX, X, and prothrombin. An INR of 4.8 is above the therapeutic range (2.0–3.0 for most; 2.5–3.5 for high-risk patients), indicating over-anticoagulation. Protamine sulfate (A) reverses heparin, not warfarin. QUESTION 08 Pharmacology

BDirect inhibition of thrombin
CEnhancement of antithrombin activity

📋 Rationale

QUESTION 10 Multiple Choice
A client receiving heparin develops a platelet count drop from 280,000 to 72,000/mm³ over 8 days and is now reporting new leg pain. The nurse suspects Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT). Which action is the priority?

BAdminister protamine sulfate immediately
CDiscontinue all heparin products immediately and notify the provider
DMonitor the platelet count daily and reassess if it drops below 50,000
📋 Rationale
C is correct. HIT is a paradoxical prothrombotic disorder caused by heparin. Despite thrombocytopenia, the client is at HIGH risk for thrombosis. QUESTION 11 Pharmacology
A client asks why they received heparin during pregnancy for DVT rather than warfarin. The nurse's best response is:
AHeparin does not cross the placental barrier, making it safe for use in pregnancy
BWarfarin has a shorter half-life and is harder to monitor during pregnancy
CHeparin has a longer therapeutic window and requires less frequent dosing
DWarfarin is contraindicated only in the third trimester of pregnancy
📋 Rationale
A is correct. Heparin is a large molecule that does not cross the placenta, making it the preferred anticoagulant during pregnancy. Warfarin is teratogenic (causes birth defects) and is contraindicated throughout pregnancy, not just in the third trimester. Heparin actually has a short half-life (~1.5 hours) — not longer. QUESTION 12 Pharmacology
A nurse is preparing to administer the heparin antidote protamine sulfate. Which administration principle is essential to prevent adverse effects?
AAdminister as a rapid IV bolus for maximum effectiveness
BGive IM in the gluteal muscle and massage well after injection
CDose is 100 mg protamine per 1 unit of heparin in the body
DAdminister by slow IV injection at no faster than 20 mg/min or 50 mg in 10 minutes
📋 Rationale
D is correct. Protamine sulfate must be given as a slow IV injection, no faster than 20 mg/min or 50 mg over 10 minutes. Rapid infusion causes hypotension, bradycardia, and cardiovascular collapse. The dosing ratio is 1 mg protamine per 100 units of heparin (not 100 mg per 1 unit as in C — that is inverted). It must be given IV, not IM. Rapid bolus (A) is dangerous.
QUESTION 13 Multiple Choice
Aspirin exerts its antiplatelet effect primarily by:
AIrreversibly blocking P2Y12 ADP receptors on platelets
BIrreversibly inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX), blocking synthesis of thromboxane A2 (TXA2)
CDirectly inhibiting thrombin formation in the coagulation cascade
DActivating antithrombin to inactivate Factor Xa
📋 Rationale
B is correct. Aspirin's antiplatelet effect comes from irreversible COX inhibition, which prevents synthesis of TXA2 (thromboxane A2) — a potent platelet activator and vasoconstrictor. Since platelets cannot synthesize new COX, this effect lasts the lifetime of the platelet (~7–10 days). Option A describes clopidogrel's mechanism. Options C and D describe mechanisms of DOACs and heparin/LMWH, respectively.
QUESTION 14 Pharmacology
A client is prescribed clopidogrel (Plavix) after coronary stent placement. Two weeks into therapy, the client develops a purpuric rash, fever, confusion, and lab results show low platelets and elevated creatinine. The nurse recognizes this as:
AHeparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)
BWarfarin-associated coagulopathy
CThrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)
DDisseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
📋 Rationale
C is correct. Clopidogrel is associated with TTP during the first 2 weeks of therapy. The classic pentad of TTP includes: thrombocytopenia, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, neurological symptoms (confusion), renal impairment (elevated creatinine), and fever. Nurses must monitor for TTP in the first 2 weeks. HIT is caused by heparin, not clopidogrel. Warfarin coagulopathy would present differently. DIC involves consumption of all clotting factors systemically.
QUESTION 15 Case Study
Clinical Scenario — STEMI
A 58-year-old male presents to the ED with crushing chest pain radiating to the left arm for 90 minutes. Diaphoresis noted. ECG shows ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF. BP 134/82, HR 102. The provider orders alteplase (tPA).

AHistory of appendectomy 3 years ago
BHistory of hemorrhagic stroke 6 months ago
CCurrent aspirin use for chronic stable angina
DAge 58 with a history of hypertension
📋 Rationale
B is correct. A recent history of hemorrhagic stroke is an absolute contraindication to fibrinolytic therapy. AGlycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor

CCOX-1 receptor
DAntithrombin III receptor

QUESTION 28 Pharmacology
A client asks why they cannot take their warfarin with spinach or kale every day. The nurse's BEST explanation is:

QUESTION 31 Pharmacology
A client's platelet count drops from 310,000 to 62,000/mm³ after 7 days of heparin therapy. The nurse understands that HIT is different from regular thrombocytopenia because HIT causes:
ADecreased clotting risk due to low platelet numbers
BBone marrow suppression leading to pancytopenia
CA paradoxical increased risk of thrombosis despite low platelets

📋 Rationale
C is correct. QUESTION 35 Multiple Choice
A client with a newly diagnosed DVT asks the nurse: "Why do I have to take both heparin and warfarin at the same time?" The nurse's best response is:
A"The two medications together are stronger than either one alone and prevent more clots."
B"Heparin is given until the DVT dissolves, then warfarin maintains blood thinning."
C"Warfarin takes 3–5 days to reach therapeutic levels, so heparin provides immediate anticoagulation during that bridge period."
D"Heparin prevents the DVT from worsening while warfarin dissolves the existing clot."
📋 Rationale
C is correct. This is the concept of "bridging therapy." Warfarin requires 3–5 days to achieve therapeutic anticoagulation because it suppresses vitamin K–dependent clotting factors that must be naturally depleted. During this delay, heparin provides immediate, reliable anticoagulation. Once the INR is therapeutic (2.0–3.0) for ≥2 consecutive days and at least 5 days of overlap, heparin is discontinued. Neither medication dissolves the existing clot (D) — anticoagulants prevent clot growth and new clot formation; fibrinolytics dissolve clots.

QUESTION 52 Multiple Choice
Protamine sulfate reverses heparin's anticoagulant effect through which mechanism?
AProtamine's positively charged groups bind ionically to heparin's negatively charged groups, forming an inactive complex
BProtamine activates antithrombin III to rapidly eliminate heparin from circulation
CProtamine restores Factor X function by competitively displacing heparin at the receptor site
DProtamine replaces vitamin K and resynthesizes clotting factors destroyed by heparin
📋 Rationale
A is correct. Protamine is a small, positively charged protein. Heparin is negatively charged.
